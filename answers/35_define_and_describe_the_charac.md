# Question 35: Define and describe the characteristics of forward and futures contracts.

**Status:** ✅ Completed | **Words:** 191 | **Generated:** 2025-07-04T00:19:00Z

---

## Answer

Both forwards and futures are derivatives that obligate the buyer to purchase, and the seller to deliver, an underlying asset at a specified future date for a price agreed today. A forward contract is a bespoke, over-the-counter agreement; its terms—quantity, quality, delivery place and date—are privately negotiated, resulting in counter-party credit risk and limited liquidity. Settlement typically occurs by physical delivery or cash difference at maturity, and no intermediate cash flows change hands before that date. A futures contract standardises these features and is traded on organised exchanges such as CME. Daily marking-to-market credits or debits gains to margin accounts, drastically reducing default risk, while the clearinghouse becomes the counter-party to every trade. Futures are highly liquid, allow easy short positions, and can be offset before expiration, but require posting initial and variation margins. Pricing of both instruments follows cost-of-carry relationships, yet forwards embed credit spreads and may face settlement risk, whereas futures prices deviate slightly due to daily margining (the convexity or timing adjustment).
