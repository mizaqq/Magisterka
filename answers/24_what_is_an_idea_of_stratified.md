# Question 24: What is an idea of stratified sampling? Is it better than an idea of simple random sampling without replacement? Justify your answer.

## Answer

Stratified sampling divides the population into non-overlapping subgroups (strata) that are internally homogeneous but differ across strata, then draws a separate simple random sample from each. The rationale is that variance within strata is smaller than variance in the total population, so combining stratum-wise estimates with appropriate weights yields a lower overall sampling variance. When stratum means differ markedly, the precision gain can be dramatic, especially for a fixed total sample size. Additional benefits include ensuring representation of small but important subpopulations and enabling domain-specific estimates such as gender or regional breakdowns. Compared with simple random sampling without replacement, stratification is superior whenever reliable auxiliary information for constructing strata is available and the within-stratum variability is indeed reduced. However, if strata are poorly defined or the characteristic of interest is uniformly distributed, the administrative costs of stratification may outweigh its modest variance reduction. Empirical design effects and Neyman allocation formulas quantify whether the stratified design's efficiency gain justifies the extra complexity. Thus, while not universally better, stratified sampling is generally preferable in heterogeneous populations because it converts known structure into statistical precision.
