## Answer

The Pure Exchange Model represents a fundamental framework in general equilibrium theory, focusing on the redistribution of fixed endowments among consumers without production. The model assumes two consumers with initial endowments of goods, seeking to maximise utility through voluntary exchange until no further mutually beneficial trades exist. The Edgeworth Box graphically illustrates this process, with dimensions representing total quantities of two goods and indifference curves showing consumer preferences. The contract curve within the box connects all Pareto-efficient allocations where marginal rates of substitution are equal between consumers. Competitive equilibrium occurs where budget constraints are tangent to indifference curves, ensuring market clearing and individual utility maximisation. The First Welfare Theorem demonstrates that competitive equilibria are Pareto-efficient, while the Second Welfare Theorem shows that any Pareto-efficient allocation can be achieved through appropriate redistribution and competitive markets. This framework provides theoretical foundation for understanding market efficiency and the role of prices in coordinating economic activity.
