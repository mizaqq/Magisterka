# Question 36: Define and describe the characteristics of standard vanilla options.

**Status:** ✅ Completed | **Words:** 181 | **Generated:** 2025-07-04T00:20:00Z

---

## Answer

A standard (vanilla) option is a derivative contract that grants the holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy (call) or sell (put) a specified underlying asset at a predetermined strike price on (European) or up to (American) a fixed expiration date. Key characteristics include: 1) Strike price, which sets the exercise level; 2) Maturity, after which the option expires worthless if unexercised; 3) Premium, an upfront non-refundable cost paid by the buyer to the writer; 4) Contract size or multiplier that scales payoff; and 5) Style (European or American) determining exercise flexibility. Payoffs are linear in the underlying beyond the strike but asymmetric—losses are capped at the premium for buyers, while writers face potentially unlimited risk. Valuation depends on factors summarised by the Black-Scholes inputs: underlying price, strike, volatility, time to maturity, risk-free rate, and dividends. Because of their simplicity and liquidity, vanilla options form the building blocks for more complex structures and are widely used for hedging, income generation, and directional speculation.
