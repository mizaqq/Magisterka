# Question 47: Describe social networks: their types, their centrality measures and their role in the market analysis.

## Answer

A social network is a graph in which nodes represent actors—people, firms or products—and edges encode relationships such as friendship, communication or co-purchase. Networks can be egocentric (focused on a single node), complete (all ties among a defined set), directed or undirected, weighted or bipartite. Centrality metrics quantify influence: degree counts direct connections; betweenness tallies how often a node lies on shortest paths, signalling brokerage power; closeness captures average distance to others, indicating rapid information reach; eigenvector and PageRank emphasise connections to already important nodes. In market analysis, these measures identify opinion leaders who seed viral campaigns, detect tightly knit consumer communities for targeted promotions, and map supply-chain interdependencies to assess systemic risk. Diffusion models layered on networks predict adoption curves, while network similarity feeds recommendation engines. Thus, social-network analytics links relational structure with economic behaviour, turning connections into actionable marketing insight.
