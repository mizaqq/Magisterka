# Question 22: Describe the objectives of the sensitivity analysis in LP problems.

## Answer

Sensitivity analysis in linear programming investigates how optimal solutions respond to small changes in model parameters. Its primary objective is to identify the allowable ranges for objective coefficients, right-hand-side constants, and shadow prices within which the current basis remains optimal. By quantifying these ranges, managers can judge the robustness of decisions to data uncertainty and prioritise data-collection efforts on the most influential parameters. It also reveals dual values, or marginal costs, which translate constraint relaxations into economic benefits—crucial for resource pricing and what-if budgeting. Another goal is to forecast the impact of introducing new activities or resources; reduced-cost analysis tells whether a candidate variable can enter the basis profitably. In practice, sensitivity outputs feed stochastic or scenario-based extensions, guiding planners to build buffers or contracts that hedge against volatility. Thus, LP sensitivity analysis transforms a single optimisation snapshot into a spectrum of near-optimal alternatives, enhancing strategic flexibility.
