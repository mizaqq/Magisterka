# Question 29: Present the characteristics/definition of massive data.

## Answer

Massive data, often called big data, refers to data sets whose size or complexity surpasses the capacity of traditional single-machine software to store, process or analyse. It is commonly characterised by the five V's: Volume (terabytes to exabytes of records), Velocity (high arrival rates demanding real-time ingestion), Variety (heterogeneous formats such as logs, video, and sensor streams), Veracity (inconsistent and noisy quality), and Value (economic insight extractable through advanced analytics). These properties require distributed storage systems like HDFS or object stores, parallel computation frameworks such as MapReduce and Spark, and columnar formats that support predicate push-down. Statistical challenges include scalable algorithms, streaming analytics and sample-efficient learning that operate in sub-linear memory. In essence, massive data is defined not by an absolute size threshold but by the need for fault-tolerant, horizontally scalable tools to transform raw bits into actionable information.
