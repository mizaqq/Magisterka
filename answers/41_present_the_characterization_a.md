# Question 41: Present the characterization and the examples of applications of the supervised and the unsupervised learning methods.

## Answer

Supervised learning infers a mapping from labelled inputs to known targets, optimising a loss such as squared error or cross-entropy; typical algorithms include linear regression, random forests and convolutional neural networks. It excels when historical examples exist: predicting house prices, classifying email spam or detecting credit-card fraud. Unsupervised learning, by contrast, seeks latent structure in unlabelled data, using objectives like cluster compactness or variance maximisation. K-means groups retail customers into segments, hierarchical clustering organises genes by expression patterns, while association-rule mining uncovers market-basket affinities. Dimensionality reduction methods such as PCA or t-SNE also fall under unsupervised learning, revealing manifolds in image or sensor streams. In practice, firms blend both paradigms—e.g., pre-clustering clients before tree-based churn prediction—to enhance model accuracy and interpretability. Thus, the key distinction is whether ground-truth labels guide the algorithm, which in turn shapes its metrics, validation techniques and real-world use cases.
