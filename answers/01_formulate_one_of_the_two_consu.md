# Question 1: Formulate one of the two consumer choice optimization problems and describe the methods used for its solution. How are the consumer preferences described?

## Answer

Consumer choice is commonly framed as a utility-maximisation problem constrained by income. Formally, the consumer maximises U(x₁, …, xₙ) subject to Σ pᵢxᵢ ≤ m, where xᵢ are chosen quantities, pᵢ their prices and m the budget. The Lagrangian method introduces multiplier λ, sets ∂L/∂xᵢ = 0 and ∂L/∂λ = 0, and solves for Marshallian demand xᵢ*(p,m). Preferences embedded in U(·) are assumed monotonic (more is better), convex (diversification preferred) and continuous, ensuring well-behaved optima. Two standard functional forms illustrate these preferences: Cobb-Douglas utility U = Π xᵢ^αᵢ, which implies constant expenditure shares, and CES utility, which allows adjustable substitution elasticity between goods. Solving the optimisation yields demand functions that describe how optimal consumption responds to price and income changes, forming the empirical backbone of consumer-demand analysis.
