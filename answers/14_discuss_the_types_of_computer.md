# Question 14: Discuss the types of computer networks.

## Answer

Computer networks are commonly classified by geographic scope into PAN, LAN, MAN, WAN, and GAN. Personal Area Networks (PAN) connect devices within a few metres—Bluetooth headsets, wearables—optimising convenience and low power usage. Local Area Networks (LAN) span rooms or buildings, employing Ethernet or Wi-Fi to deliver high bandwidth and low latency for organisational intranets. Metropolitan Area Networks (MAN) cover a city or campus using fibre rings or microwave links, aggregating traffic from multiple LANs with regional redundancy. Wide Area Networks (WAN) interconnect LANs across countries via leased lines, MPLS, or the public Internet, trading latency for global reach; the Internet itself can be viewed as a massive federated WAN. Global Area Networks (GAN) such as satellite constellations provide worldwide connectivity where terrestrial infrastructure is absent. Alternative taxonomies consider topology (bus, star, mesh) or function (storage, sensor, ad-hoc), but geographic scope remains the primary lens for capacity planning and protocol selection.
