# Question 37: Define and describe the characteristics of exotic options.

---

## Answer

Exotic options are non-standard derivatives whose payoff depends on complex features beyond simple strike and expiry. Categories include: 1) Path-dependent options like Asian (payoff based on average price) and lookback (uses maximum or minimum price) that reduce manipulation risk or confer hindsight benefits. 2) Barrier options, activated or extinguished when the underlying crosses a preset level, cheaper for hedgers who view that event as unlikely. 3) Digital or binary options that pay a fixed amount if in-the-money, creating all-or-nothing exposure. 4) Compound options, which grant the right to buy another option, useful in staged project valuation. Exotics may embed multiple underlyings (rainbow options) or customised settlement schedules. They trade over the counter, so terms, valuation models, and liquidity vary widely, and counter-party credit risk is greater than for exchange-listed vanilla options. Pricing often requires Monte Carlo simulation or lattice methods because closed-form formulas rarely exist. Exotics enable precise risk transfer but demand sophisticated management and are typically used by corporates and structured-product desks rather than retail investors.
